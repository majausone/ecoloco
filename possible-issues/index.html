Possible issues in Virtual Ecosystem 0.2.2

While porting Virtual Ecosystem to JavaScript, bit for bit, we found a few places where the code seems not to do what was intended. We list them here in case they are useful to its authors. They are possible issues: we may well be misreading the model, and we would be glad to be corrected.

Everything refers to virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2 (commit 0176dc2). Each snippet is self-contained and was run with that version (Python 3.12, its own virtual environment); the output shown under it is the real output.

Our JavaScript engine reproduces the original exactly, including these behaviours. The fixes are optional: in the engine page they are all switched on together by the «fix the herbivore bug» box, and off by default, so that the engine gives the same bits as the original.

  • 1. Likely bugs
    1. The metabolic rate uses the Boltzmann constant in J/K where the formula needs eV/K
    2. Foraging time is truncated to whole days
    3. Reproductive mass never increases
    4. Enum members compared with strings
    5. Understorey biomass in kg m⁻² is read as kg per cell
    6. What animals eat from the understorey is never removed from it
    7. Each cohort metabolises once for every cell of its territory
    8. Foraging time is divided by all diet categories, but all plants (or all prey) are eaten in one call
    9. Mushroom eaters are never offered fungal fruiting bodies
    10. Adults with indirect development also metamorphose
    11. Initial cohorts are counted as adults but created as newborns
    12. Plant and litter resources are not depleted between cohorts in the same step
    13. Damuth’s law uses its intercept without the logarithm
    14. Canopy herbivory of a whole cohort seems to be subtracted from per-stem masses
    15. The water limitation factor can be negative
    16. Soil evaporation uses vapour pressure instead of specific humidity
    17. Eaten fruiting bodies (kg) are subtracted from a pool in kg m⁻²
    18. A litter pool with a decay rate of zero becomes NaN
  • 2. Things that only show with a daily time step
    1. No animal ever eats
    2. Rain is lost on about 70 % of days
  • 3. Suggestions: parameters and design choices
    1. Assimilation efficiency
    2. Body C:N:P proportions
    3. Prey size tolerance
    4. Breeding threshold
    5. Prey density counted twice in the encounter rate
    6. A predator always kills at least one whole prey
    7. Minimum prey mass of 0.1 g
    8. Metabolism is paid only from body mass
    9. Each grid is a closed system
    10. The number of cohorts grows without limit
    11. Search rates (our least certain change)

1. Likely bugs

Places where the code seems to do something different from what its own documentation, units or variable names say. We are fairly confident about these, but you know the model far better than we do.

1.1 The metabolic rate uses the Boltzmann constant in J/K where the formula needs eV/K

What the original does
The activation energy Ea = 0.69 is in eV, but k_B defaults to CoreConstants().boltzmann_constant, which is scipy.constants.Boltzmann in J/K (1.38·10⁻²³).
Where
models/animal/scaling_functions.py, metabolic_rate()
with exp(-(Ea / (kB * Tk)))
Why it looks like a problem
With k_B in J/K the exponent is about −1.6·10²⁰, so the temperature term is exactly 0.0 and every metabolic rate is 0.
Reproduce it
from math import exp
from virtual_ecosystem.core.model_config import CoreConstants
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.model_config import AnimalConstants
Es, Ea = AnimalConstants().metabolic_scaling_coefficients
kB = CoreConstants().boltzmann_constant
print(kB, Ea)
print(exp(-(Ea / (kB * 310.0))))              # as in metabolic_rate()
print(exp(-(Ea / (8.617333262e-5 * 310.0))))  # with k_B in eV/K
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
1.380649e-23 0.69
0.0
6.059394106690683e-12
Effect
Animals never spend energy: total_animal_respiration is exactly 0 in every step of every run.
What our fix does
Use k_B = 8.617333262·10⁻⁵ eV/K in the metabolic rate. switch: boltzmann_ev

1.2 Foraging time is truncated to whole days

What the original does
The time available per diet is computed with numpy.timedelta64 arithmetic: dt * tau_f * sigma_f_t / diet_category_count.
Where
models/animal/animal_cohorts.py, AnimalCohort.forage_cohort()
time_available_per_diet
Why it looks like a problem
Multiplying or dividing a timedelta64 in days by a float rounds down to whole days.
Reproduce it
import numpy as np
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.model_config import AnimalConstants
tau_f = AnimalConstants().tau_f      # 0.5
print(np.timedelta64(30, "D") * tau_f * 1.0 / 2)   # monthly step, 2 diet categories: 7.5 days expected
print(np.timedelta64(1, "D") * tau_f * 1.0 / 3)    # daily step, 3 diet categories
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
7 days
0 days
Effect
With a monthly step part of each day is lost; with a daily step the time is 0 days and no animal ever eats (see section 2).
What our fix does
Compute the time as a float number of days. switch: forrajeo_continuo

1.3 Reproductive mass never increases

What the original does
reproductive_mass_cnp starts at zero and the only call that changes it subtracts the mass of the offspring. Growth always goes to mass_cnp. The constant flow_to_reproductive_mass_threshold is defined in the configuration but not used anywhere.
Where
models/animal/animal_cohorts.py, AnimalCohort.grow()
models/animal/animal_model.py, AnimalModel.handle_post_birth_parent_updates()
Why it looks like a problem
The number of offspring is computed from the reproductive mass, so it is always 0.
Reproduce it
import inspect
import virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_cohorts as ac
import virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_model as am
lines = (inspect.getsource(ac) + inspect.getsource(am)).splitlines()
calls = [lines[i + 1].strip() for i, l in enumerate(lines) if "reproductive_mass_cnp.update(" in l]
print(len(calls), "call(s) to reproductive_mass_cnp.update():", calls)
src = inspect.getsource(ac) + inspect.getsource(am)
print("flow_to_reproductive_mass_threshold used:", "flow_to_reproductive_mass_threshold" in src)
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
1 call(s) to reproductive_mass_cnp.update(): ['C=-min(total_c, parent.reproductive_mass_cnp.C),']
flow_to_reproductive_mass_threshold used: False
Effect
No animal is ever born.
What our fix does
Once a cohort is adult (mass ≥ flow_to_reproductive_mass_threshold × adult mass), what it grows goes to its reproductive mass. switch: reproduccion

1.4 Enum members compared with strings

What the original does
Several conditions compare functional_group.reproductive_type or migration_type (plain Enum members) with string literals.
Where
models/animal/animal_model.py, handle_post_birth_parent_updates() (== "semelparous")
models/animal/animal_model.py, calculate_semelparous_mass_loss() (!= "semelparous")
models/animal/animal_model.py, birth_community() (!= "nonreproductive")
models/animal/animal_model.py, migrate_external_community() (== "seasonal")
Why it looks like a problem
An Enum member is never equal to a string, so the equalities are always False and the inequalities always True.
Reproduce it
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_traits import ReproductiveType, MigrationType
print(ReproductiveType.SEMELPAROUS == "semelparous")
print(MigrationType.SEASONAL == "seasonal")
print(ReproductiveType.NONREPRODUCTIVE != "nonreproductive")
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
False
False
True
Effect
Semelparous parents never lose their mass or die after breeding, no cohort ever migrates seasonally, and non-reproductive groups are treated as reproductive.
What our fix does
Compare with the enum members (ReproductiveType.SEMELPAROUS, …). switch: comparar_enums

1.5 Understorey biomass in kg m⁻² is read as kg per cell

What the original does
In ARRAY_RESOURCES, the litter and fungal pools are declared with density=True (converted to mass with the cell area), but subcanopy_vegetation_cnp and subcanopy_seedbank_cnp are not, although their unit is kg m⁻².
Where
models/animal/array_resources.py, ARRAY_RESOURCES
Why it looks like a problem
Without the conversion, a cell of 8100 m² offers herbivores the biomass of one square metre.
Reproduce it
import tomllib, importlib.resources as ir
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.array_resources import ARRAY_RESOURCES
dv = tomllib.loads((ir.files("virtual_ecosystem") / "data_variables.toml").read_text(encoding="utf-8"))
unit = {v["name"]: v.get("unit") for v in dv["variable"]}
for r in ARRAY_RESOURCES:
    print(r.pool_array, "|", unit.get(r.pool_array), "| density =", r.density)
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
subcanopy_vegetation_cnp | kg m-2 | density = False
subcanopy_seedbank_cnp | kg m2 | density = False
canopy_foliage_cnp | kg | density = False
canopy_seed_cnp | kg | density = False
canopy_fruit_cnp | kg | density = False
fallen_seeds_cnp | kg | density = False
fallen_fruit_cnp | kg | density = False
litter_pool_above_metabolic_cnp | kg m^-2 | density = True
litter_pool_above_structural_cnp | kg m^-2 | density = True
litter_pool_woody_cnp | kg m^-2 | density = True
litter_pool_below_metabolic_cnp | kg m^-2 | density = True
litter_pool_below_structural_cnp | kg m^-2 | density = True
fungal_fruiting_bodies_cnp | kg m^-2 | density = True
Effect
Ground herbivores (insects, lizards) find thousands of times less food, and because the encounter rate uses the biomass density squared, their intake is many millions of times lower.
What our fix does
Treat both pools as densities (multiply by the cell area). switch: sotobosque_por_m2

1.6 What animals eat from the understorey is never removed from it

What the original does
The animal model writes subcanopy_vegetation_cnp_consumed and subcanopy_seedbank_cnp_consumed. The plant model lists and initialises them, but never subtracts them (it does subtract the canopy and fallen fruit and seed consumption).
Where
models/plants/plants_model.py, PlantsModel (vars_required_for_update)
models/plants/plants_model.py, PlantsModel.apply_herbivory()
Why it looks like a problem
Consumption that is recorded but never applied makes the understorey an endless resource.
Reproduce it
import inspect
import virtual_ecosystem.models.plants.plants_model as pm
lines = [l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(pm).splitlines() if "subcanopy_vegetation_cnp_consumed" in l]
print(len(lines), "mentions in the plant model:", lines)
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
2 mentions in the plant model: ['"subcanopy_vegetation_cnp_consumed",', '"subcanopy_vegetation_cnp_consumed",']
Effect
The understorey is never depleted by herbivores.
What our fix does
Subtract the consumed understorey biomass in the plant update. switch: restar_sotobosque

1.7 Each cohort metabolises once for every cell of its territory

What the original does
A cohort is appended to the community of every cell of its territory, and metabolize_community loops over cells and then over the cohorts in each community.
Where
models/animal/animal_model.py, update_community_occupancy()
models/animal/animal_model.py, metabolize_community()
Why it looks like a problem
A cohort whose territory covers n cells calls metabolize() n times per step.
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_model import AnimalModel
for f in (AnimalModel.update_community_occupancy, AnimalModel.metabolize_community):
    print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(f).splitlines()
           if l.strip().startswith(("for ", "self.communities[cell_id].append", "metabolic_waste_mass ="))])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['for cell_id in territory_cells:', 'self.communities[cell_id].append(cohort)']
['for cell_id, community in self.communities.items():', 'for cohort in community:', 'metabolic_waste_mass = cohort.metabolize(cohort.current_temperature, dt)']
Effect
Once the Boltzmann constant is fixed, large animals (territories of up to 81 cells) spend up to 81 times their metabolism and starve within days.
What our fix does
Metabolise each cohort once per step and spread its waste and respiration over its territory. switch: metabolismo_una_vez

1.8 Foraging time is divided by all diet categories, but all plants (or all prey) are eaten in one call

What the original does
diet_category_count counts each category (foliage, fruit, seeds…), the time is divided by it, and then the whole plant list is foraged in one call with that single share. The same happens with prey.
Where
models/animal/animal_cohorts.py, AnimalCohort.forage_cohort()
Why it looks like a problem
A herbivore that eats foliage, fruit and seeds gets one third of its time for all plants together.
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_traits import DietType
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_cohorts import AnimalCohort
print((DietType.FOLIAGE | DietType.FRUIT | DietType.SEEDS).count_dietary_categories())
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(AnimalCohort.forage_cohort).splitlines() if "time_available_per_diet" in l])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
3
['time_available_per_diet = (', 'adjusted_dt=time_available_per_diet,', 'adjusted_dt=time_available_per_diet,', 'adjusted_dt=time_available_per_diet,', 'adjusted_dt=time_available_per_diet,', 'adjusted_dt=time_available_per_diet,', 'adjusted_dt=time_available_per_diet,', 'adjusted_dt=time_available_per_diet,', 'adjusted_dt=time_available_per_diet,', 'adjusted_dt=time_available_per_diet,']
Effect
Herbivores and predators with several diet categories forage for only a fraction of the intended time.
What our fix does
Give the plant (or prey) call the time of all the plant (or prey) categories of the diet. switch: tiempo_plantas, tiempo_presas

1.9 Mushroom eaters are never offered fungal fruiting bodies

What the original does
fungal_fruiting_bodies_cnp is defined as an array resource with diet_type=MUSHROOMS, but forage_community only builds the array resource list for plant and detritus diets.
Where
models/animal/animal_model.py, AnimalModel.forage_community()
Why it looks like a problem
The MUSHROOMS flag is never checked, so the resource never reaches the cohorts.
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_model import AnimalModel
code = inspect.getsource(AnimalModel.forage_community).split('"""')[2]   # without the docstring
print("DietType.MUSHROOMS checked:", "DietType.MUSHROOMS" in code)
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
DietType.MUSHROOMS checked: False
Effect
Fungivores never eat mushrooms.
What our fix does
Add the MUSHROOMS flag to the array resource request. switch: setas

1.10 Adults with indirect development also metamorphose

What the original does
A cohort metamorphoses when its development type is indirect and its mass reaches the adult mass; its development status (larval or adult) is not checked.
Where
models/animal/animal_model.py, AnimalModel.metamorphose_community()
Why it looks like a problem
An adult butterfly that reaches its adult mass is turned into its offspring group (caterpillars).
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_model import AnimalModel
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(AnimalModel.metamorphose_community).splitlines()
       if "development_type" in l or "development_status" in l or "mass_current >=" in l])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['cohort.functional_group.development_type == DevelopmentType.INDIRECT', 'and (cohort.mass_current >= cohort.functional_group.adult_mass)']
Effect
Butterflies turn back into caterpillars and never breed.
What our fix does
Only larval cohorts metamorphose. switch: metamorfosis_solo_larvas

1.11 Initial cohorts are counted as adults but created as newborns

What the original does
The number of individuals comes from the biomass density divided by the adult mass, but every cohort is created with mass=fg.birth_mass.
Where
models/animal/animal_model.py, AnimalModel._initialize_communities()
Why it looks like a problem
The starting biomass is a few per cent of what was intended, and almost no animal reaches breeding size.
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_model import AnimalModel
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(AnimalModel._initialize_communities).splitlines()
       if "biomass_density_to_individuals" in l or "fg.adult_mass," in l or "mass=fg.birth_mass" in l])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['total_individuals = biomass_density_to_individuals(', 'fg.adult_mass,', 'mass=fg.birth_mass,']
Effect
Populations start as newborns (4–10 % of adult mass) and most die before breeding.
What our fix does
Spread the initial cohorts between birth and adult mass. switch: cohortes_iniciales_mezcladas

1.12 Plant and litter resources are not depleted between cohorts in the same step

What the original does
Each time a cohort asks for a cell resource, ResourcePool.__getitem__ builds a new CellResource from elemental_masses, which are not reduced when something is eaten; get_eaten only reduces the temporary object.
Where
models/animal/array_resources.py, ResourcePool.__getitem__()
CellResource.get_eaten()
Why it looks like a problem
Every cohort sees the full pool, so the total eaten in a step can exceed what is there.
Reproduce it
import inspect
import virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.array_resources as ar
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(ar.ResourcePool.__getitem__).splitlines() if "available_elemental_masses" in l])
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(ar.CellResource.get_eaten).splitlines() if "-=" in l])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['available_elemental_masses=self.elemental_masses[cell_id],']
['self._mass_current -= actual']
Effect
With many herbivores, consumption can exceed the available biomass.
What our fix does
Subtract what each cohort eats from the pool before the next one forages (and never take more than half of what is left). switch: agotar_recursos

1.13 Damuth’s law uses its intercept without the logarithm

What the original does
Damuth’s law is log₁₀ D = a + b log₁₀ M, but damuths_law uses a as a multiplier: D = a · Mb. (Only with density_scaling_method = "damuth"; the default is "madingley".)
Where
models/animal/scaling_functions.py, damuths_law()
Why it looks like a problem
With a = 4.23 the densities are 104.23/4.23 ≈ 4000 times too low.
Reproduce it
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.scaling_functions import damuths_law
print(damuths_law(10.0, (-0.75, 4.23)) * 1e6, "individuals per km2 for a 10 kg herbivore")
print(10 ** 4.23 * (10.0 * 1000) ** -0.75, "with log10 D = 4.23 - 0.75 log10 M (M in g)")
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
0.00423 individuals per km2 for a 10 kg herbivore
16.982436524617462 with log10 D = 4.23 - 0.75 log10 M (M in g)
Effect
Unrealistic starting densities when the Damuth method is chosen.
What our fix does
Use 10a. switch: damuth_log10

1.14 Canopy herbivory of a whole cohort seems to be subtracted from per-stem masses

What the original does
Herbivory is shared among the cohorts of a PFT and subtracted from each cohort’s tissue masses and from stem_allometry.foliage_mass, which we understand to be per stem.
Where
models/plants/plants_model.py, PlantsModel.apply_herbivory()
Why it looks like a problem
If those masses are per stem, a cohort of many stems loses its whole consumption from each stem.
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.plants.plants_model import PlantsModel
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(PlantsModel.apply_herbivory).splitlines()
       if "relative_herbivory" in l or "apply_herbivory(" in l or "foliage_mass -" in l])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['def apply_herbivory(self) -> None:', 'relative_herbivory = tissue.get_relative_carbon_biomass_by_pft(', '* relative_herbivory[:, np.newaxis]', 'tissue.apply_herbivory(herbivory_by_cohort)', 'community.stem_allometry.foliage_mass - foliage_carbon_loss / 2']
Effect
With many herbivores, crowns drop to zero and later steps divide 0 by 0. We may be misreading the units here.
What our fix does
Share the consumption among the stems of the cohort. switch: hojas_comidas_por_tallo

1.15 The water limitation factor can be negative

What the original does
The factor is min(1, (soil_moisture − residual) / demand), with no lower bound.
Where
models/plants/plants_model.py, PlantsModel.apply_water_limitation()
Why it looks like a problem
When the soil is drier than the residual water, the factor is negative, and so is production.
Reproduce it
import numpy as np
soil_moisture, residual, demand = np.array([20.0]), np.array([30.0]), np.array([50.0])
print(np.minimum(1, (soil_moisture - residual) / demand))   # as in apply_water_limitation()
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
[-0.2]
Effect
Negative gross primary production in dry soils.
What our fix does
Bound the factor at 0. switch: agua_sin_negativos

1.16 Soil evaporation uses vapour pressure instead of specific humidity

What the original does
The docstring says E = ρ/Rₐ (α q_sat − q). The code computes saturated_specific_humidity but uses saturation_vapour_pressure (kPa) in the flux, and then divides the mass flux (kg m⁻² s⁻¹) by the latent heat of vaporisation.
Where
models/hydrology/above_ground.py, calculate_soil_evaporation()
Why it looks like a problem
The two quantities differ by two orders of magnitude, and a mass flux divided by latent heat is no longer a mass flux.
Reproduce it
import inspect
import virtual_ecosystem.models.hydrology.above_ground as ag
s = inspect.getsource(ag.calculate_soil_evaporation)
print([l.strip() for l in s.splitlines() if "alpha * saturation_vapour_pressure" in l or "evaporative_flux / latent_heat_vapourisation" in l])
print("saturated_specific_humidity used in the flux:", "alpha * saturated_specific_humidity" in s)
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['alpha * saturation_vapour_pressure - specific_humidity_air', 'evaporative_flux / latent_heat_vapourisation']
saturated_specific_humidity used in the flux: False
Effect
Soil evaporation does not follow the documented formula.
What our fix does
Use α q_sat − q and do not divide by the latent heat (kg m⁻² s⁻¹ = mm s⁻¹). switch: evaporacion_suelo

1.17 Eaten fruiting bodies (kg) are subtracted from a pool in kg m⁻²

What the original does
The soil model subtracts fungal_fruiting_bodies_consumed_cnp (unit kg) from fungal_fruiting_bodies_cnp (unit kg m⁻²).
Where
models/soil/soil_model.py, SoilModel._update()
Why it looks like a problem
The units do not match: the subtraction is 8100 times too large in a 8100 m² cell.
Reproduce it
import tomllib, importlib.resources as ir
dv = tomllib.loads((ir.files("virtual_ecosystem") / "data_variables.toml").read_text(encoding="utf-8"))
unit = {v["name"]: v.get("unit") for v in dv["variable"]}
print("fungal_fruiting_bodies_cnp:", unit["fungal_fruiting_bodies_cnp"])
print("fungal_fruiting_bodies_consumed_cnp:", unit["fungal_fruiting_bodies_consumed_cnp"])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
fungal_fruiting_bodies_cnp: kg m^-2
fungal_fruiting_bodies_consumed_cnp: kg
Effect
Fruiting bodies can become negative once animals eat them.
What our fix does
Divide the consumed mass by the cell area before subtracting it. switch: setas_por_m2

1.18 A litter pool with a decay rate of zero becomes NaN

What the original does
calculate_final_pool_size computes the equilibrium as input_rate / decay_rate.
Where
models/litter/carbon.py, calculate_final_pool_size()
Why it looks like a problem
With a zero rate (for instance very dry or cold conditions) this is ∞ − ∞.
Reproduce it
import numpy as np
from virtual_ecosystem.models.litter.carbon import calculate_final_pool_size
with np.errstate(all="ignore"):
    print(calculate_final_pool_size(np.array([0.01]), np.array([0.0]), np.array([1.0]), 1.0))
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
[nan]
Effect
A NaN that spreads to the rest of the litter and soil.
What our fix does
With a zero rate, the pool just adds its input. switch: tasa_cero

2. Things that only show with a daily time step

The example runs monthly. Running it daily (update_interval = "1 day") shows two more problems.

2.1 No animal ever eats

What the original does
The truncation of the foraging time described in section 1 gives 0 days when the step is one day.
Where
models/animal/animal_cohorts.py, AnimalCohort.forage_cohort()
Why it looks like a problem
One day × tau_f (0.5) × sigma_f_t / number of diet categories is less than one day, and it is rounded down.
Reproduce it
import numpy as np
print(np.timedelta64(1, "D") * 0.5 * 1.0 / 1)   # even with a single diet category
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
0 days
Effect
At a daily step, no animal eats and all of them starve.
What our fix does
As above. switch: forrajeo_continuo

2.2 Rain is lost on about 70 % of days

What the original does
distribute_monthly_rainfall spreads each step’s rain over its days with a Markov chain whose first day is wet with probability p_wet_dry (0.3). With a one-day step, every day is a «first day».
Where
models/hydrology/above_ground.py, distribute_monthly_rainfall()
Why it looks like a problem
The real daily rainfall from the input data is kept only when the draw says the day is wet.
Reproduce it
import numpy as np
from virtual_ecosystem.models.hydrology.above_ground import distribute_monthly_rainfall
dry = sum(distribute_monthly_rainfall(np.array([10.0]), 1, 0.6, 0.3, 1.0, 1.0, seed=s)[0, 0] == 0
          for s in range(1000))
print(dry / 1000, "of 1000 rainy days come out dry")
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
0.719 of 1000 rainy days come out dry
Effect
The soil dries out, and in our runs the litter model then produced NaN.
What our fix does
With a one-day step, use each day’s rain as it is. switch: lluvia_paso_diario

3. Suggestions: parameters and design choices

These are not bugs. They are values or choices that, once the problems above were fixed, did not let the Maliau animals sustain themselves in our runs. We changed them by our own judgement; we offer them only as suggestions.

3.1 Assimilation efficiency

What the original does
conversion_efficiency is 0.1 for herbivores, 0.25 for carnivores and 0.175 for omnivores.
Where
models/animal/model_config.py, AnimalConstants.conversion_efficiency
Why it looks like a problem
These look like trophic transfer efficiencies, which already include respiration; with metabolism subtracted separately, respiration may be counted twice.
Reproduce it
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.model_config import AnimalConstants
print({k.name: v for k, v in AnimalConstants().conversion_efficiency.items()})
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
{'HERBIVORE': 0.1, 'CARNIVORE': 0.25, 'OMNIVORE': 0.175}
Effect
Animals assimilate too little of what they eat to cover their metabolism.
What our fix does
We use Madingley’s assimilation efficiencies (Harfoot et al. 2014): 0.5 herbivores, 0.8 carnivores, 0.65 omnivores. parameter change

3.2 Body C:N:P proportions

What the original does
The body C:N:P proportions are 50/30/20 for mammals, 40/30/30 for birds and 40/20/40 for invertebrates, amphibians and reptiles (20–40 % phosphorus).
Where
models/animal/model_config.py, AnimalConstants.cnp_proportion_terms
Why it looks like a problem
A real animal body is roughly 80/17/3 (insects about 82/16/2): phosphorus is a few per cent, not a fifth or more.
Reproduce it
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.model_config import AnimalConstants
for taxa, cnp in AnimalConstants().cnp_proportion_terms.items():
    print(taxa.name, cnp)
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
MAMMAL {'C': 0.5, 'N': 0.3, 'P': 0.2}
BIRD {'C': 0.4, 'N': 0.3, 'P': 0.3}
INVERTEBRATE {'C': 0.4, 'N': 0.2, 'P': 0.4}
AMPHIBIAN {'C': 0.4, 'N': 0.2, 'P': 0.4}
REPTILE {'C': 0.4, 'N': 0.2, 'P': 0.4}
Effect
Growth tends to be limited by phosphorus and nitrogen.
What our fix does
80/17/3 for vertebrates and 82/16/2 for invertebrates. parameter change

3.3 Prey size tolerance

What the original does
sigma_opt_pred_prey = 0.7.
Where
models/animal/model_config.py, AnimalConstants.sigma_opt_pred_prey
Why it looks like a problem
With 0.7, a 100 g bird hardly «sees» half-gram insects; real predators take prey of very different sizes.
Reproduce it
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.model_config import AnimalConstants
print(AnimalConstants().sigma_opt_pred_prey)
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
0.7
Effect
Predators find very little prey.
What our fix does
2.0. parameter change

3.4 Breeding threshold

What the original does
birth_mass_threshold = 1.5: a cohort breeds when its reproductive mass reaches half its body mass, and releases all the young at once.
Where
models/animal/model_config.py, AnimalConstants.birth_mass_threshold
Why it looks like a problem
A 1 kg bird with 40 g chicks would release 12 at once.
Reproduce it
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.model_config import AnimalConstants
print(AnimalConstants().birth_mass_threshold)
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
1.5
Effect
Few, very large broods.
What our fix does
1.1 (10 %), which gives 2–3 young. parameter change

3.5 Prey density counted twice in the encounter rate

What the original does
k_i_j multiplies the prey cohort density by the density of its mass bin.
Where
models/animal/scaling_functions.py, k_i_j()
Why it looks like a problem
The docstring says this is intentional and absorbed by calibration; we found encounters proportional to density easier to calibrate.
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal import scaling_functions as sf
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(sf.k_i_j).splitlines() if l.strip().startswith("return")])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['return alpha_i_j * (N_j_t / intersection_area_ha) * theta_i_j']
Effect
Encounters scale with the square of prey density.
What our fix does
Linear in prey density. switch: caza_lineal

3.6 A predator always kills at least one whole prey

What the original does
get_eaten uses ceil for the number of individuals killed.
Where
models/animal/animal_cohorts.py, AnimalCohort.get_eaten()
Why it looks like a problem
A predator that only needs a small fraction of a prey kills a whole one each step.
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_cohorts import AnimalCohort
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(AnimalCohort.get_eaten).splitlines() if "ceil(" in l])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['max_individuals_killed = ceil(potential_consumed_mass / individual_mass)']
Effect
Predation is biased upwards for small intakes.
What our fix does
Round at random, with the same mean. switch: caza_redondeo

3.7 Minimum prey mass of 0.1 g

What the original does
prey_group_selection gives every prey group a (0.0001, 1000) kg range.
Where
models/animal/scaling_functions.py, prey_group_selection()
Why it looks like a problem
Termites and young insects weigh less than 0.1 g.
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal import scaling_functions as sf
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(sf.prey_group_selection).splitlines() if "0.0001" in l][:1])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['result[fg.name] = (0.0001, 1000.0)']
Effect
Small invertebrates cannot be eaten.
What our fix does
A much lower minimum (one microgram). switch: presas_pequenas

3.8 Metabolism is paid only from body mass

What the original does
metabolize takes the metabolic cost from the cohort’s body carbon.
Where
models/animal/animal_cohorts.py, AnimalCohort.metabolize()
Why it looks like a problem
Animals usually pay their metabolism first from what they have just eaten.
Reproduce it
import inspect
from virtual_ecosystem.models.animal.animal_cohorts import AnimalCohort
print([l.strip() for l in inspect.getsource(AnimalCohort.metabolize).splitlines() if "mass_cnp.update" in l])
Output with virtual_ecosystem 0.2.2:
['self.mass_cnp.update(C=-actual_carbon_metabolized)']
Effect
Animals lose body mass even when they have eaten enough.
What our fix does
Use the surplus carbon from the food first. switch: metabolismo_con_comida

3.9 Each grid is a closed system

What the original does
No animal enters or leaves the simulated area.
Where
models/animal/animal_model.py
Why it looks like a problem
A small patch of forest is usually connected to the forest around it.
Effect
Rare large animals go extinct locally and cannot come back.
What our fix does
If a population falls below half of its starting density, animals come in from the edge; above twice, they leave. switch: inmigracion

3.10 The number of cohorts grows without limit

What the original does
Every animal birth and every plant recruitment creates a new cohort, and cohorts are never merged.
Where
models/animal/animal_model.py, AnimalModel.handle_post_birth_parent_updates()
models/plants/plants_model.py, apply_recruitment()
Why it looks like a problem
Over long runs the number of cohorts, and the run time, keep growing.
Effect
Long simulations get slower and slower.
What our fix does
Merge similar animal cohorts when a group has more than 60 (as Madingley does), and add new seedlings to the seedling cohort of their type. switch: fusionar_cohortes, reclutas_juntos

3.11 Search rates (our least certain change)

What the original does
Even with everything above, the encounter formulas gave intakes that did not match real animals for the Maliau groups: some never ate and others ate everything.
Where
Our Maliau scenario only, not the original code
Why it looks like a problem
We added an optional search_rate_multiplier column to the functional group table and calibrated it so each group assimilates about 1.5 times what it spends.
Effect
The multipliers range from about 0.000003 (termites) to 260,000 (scavengers), which suggests the encounter formulas themselves may not scale with real densities and body masses. This is the change we are least sure about.
What our fix does
A per-group multiplier of the search rate (1 = original). switch: search_rate_multiplier
